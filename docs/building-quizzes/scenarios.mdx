# Result scenarios <PlanBadge plan="basic" />

A scenario is a result page you write yourself, attached to a precise trio of products. By the end
of this page your quiz answers its winning combinations with a title, a description, an image and
tips, instead of a bare list of products.

## Before you start

- A quiz whose answers already vote for products, see
  [Product matching](/docs/building-quizzes/product-matching).
- A store on the Basic plan or above.

## What a scenario is

A scenario carries a title, a description, a desktop and a mobile image, a list of tips, and the
products it recommends, each with an optional label. When a customer finishes the quiz and their
winning products are exactly the products of a scenario, that scenario is the result page they see.

That is what a scenario is for: the quiz works out which products fit, and the scenario turns that
list into a recommendation in your own words. The customer reads why these products were chosen and
how to use them together, rather than three product cards with nothing around them.

## The plan you need

Scenarios are available on the Basic plan and above. On the Free plan, the **Scenarios** tab shows
a banner titled **Editorial scenarios are available on the Basic plan and above**, which reads
**Upgrade to unlock editorial scenarios: rich result pages with custom copy, images, and tips for
each product trio.** and carries an **Upgrade plan** button. **Add scenario** is disabled, with the
tooltip **Scenario limit reached for your plan**.

From Basic upwards there is no limit on the number of scenarios a quiz can
hold. The plan decides whether scenarios are available at all, not how many you
write.

## Create a scenario

1. Open the quiz and select its **Scenarios** tab.
2. Select **Add scenario** at the bottom of the left sidebar.

The **Questions** tab offers the same from its own sidebar: its **SCENARIOS** section carries an
**Add scenario** button and a **Manage scenarios →** button that opens the tab. On the Free plan
that first button reads **Unlock scenarios →** and opens the page where you change plans instead.

A new scenario arrives named **New scenario**, at the end of the list, with no description, no tip,
no product and no image. It is written to the quiz straight away, which is why **Add scenario** is
disabled while you have unsaved changes, with the tooltip **Save or discard your changes first**.

There is no way to duplicate a single scenario. Duplicating the quiz copies all
of them, along with the rest of the quiz.

## Write the scenario

Select the scenario in the left sidebar. Its settings open in the panel on the right, in four
sections.

**General** holds the **Title** and the **Description**. The title heads the editorial part of the
result page, and the description is the narrative under it. The description's help text states
where it lands: **Displayed on the result page.**

**Tips** holds the short pieces of advice listed under the description. Select **Add tip** to add
one: it arrives reading **New tip**. Each tip carries a text and one of three icons, **General**,
**Morning** or **Evening**, and the icon is printed next to the text on the result page.

**Products** holds the products this scenario recommends. Select **Add product** to pick them in
the Shopify product picker. Each product row ends with a label field, whose placeholder reads
**Custom label**: the label is printed above the product's name on its card, and is how a trio
tells the customer which product plays which role.

**Images** holds **Desktop image**, with the help text **Shown on screens wider than 768 px**, and
**Mobile image**, with the help text **Falls back to desktop image if empty**. The image
illustrates the result page, above the scenario's title.

No field has a length limit, and there is no limit on the number of tips. Write what the
recommendation needs.

Everything in this panel is held until you save it. A bar appears at the top of the screen as soon
as something changes, one **Save** writes every pending change on the tab at once, and **Discard**
reverts them all.

## The trio

This is the section that decides whether your scenario is ever shown, so read it before writing a
dozen scenarios.

When the customer answers the last question, the quiz counts the votes their answers gave and keeps
the three products with the most votes. A scenario is shown only when its products are exactly
those three: the same products, and the same number of products. A close match does not count.
Sharing two products out of three does not count. Either the two lists are identical, or the
scenario stays unused.

Three consequences follow:

- A scenario carrying four products or more is never shown. The quiz never designates more than
  three winners, so its list can never equal the scenario's.
- A scenario carrying one or two products is shown only when the quiz itself designates one or two
  winners, which happens only when fewer than three products collected votes. In a quiz with
  products attached to its answers, that is rare.
- Two scenarios carrying the same products are both a match, and the order of the list breaks the
  tie, see the reordering section below.

The app warns you about the second case: a scenario holding fewer than three products shows a
banner reading **Incomplete trios rarely match: visitors will see the generic fallback.**

So compose one scenario per combination the quiz can actually produce. Which combinations those are
follows from the votes and exclusions on your answers, see
[Product matching](/docs/building-quizzes/product-matching): work out which trios your answers can
realistically bring to the top, and write a scenario for each of those, rather than for every trio
your catalogue could theoretically form.

## When no scenario matches

The customer still gets their result. The page shows the quiz's generic result title and subtitle,
and the products their answers picked, laid out by your result screen settings. What is missing
is everything a scenario would have added: no description, no tips, no image.

This is the normal behaviour of the quiz, not an error. Every combination you did not write a
scenario for ends here, and the response is still recorded, as a generic result.

## Reorder the scenarios

Drag a scenario by the handle at the start of its row, which carries the tooltip **Drag to reorder**,
and drop it where you want in the list. The new order is held by the save bar like any other
change: select **Save** to write it.

The order has one effect. If two scenarios carry the same products, the one higher in the list is
the one shown. In every other case the order changes nothing for the customer: the trio selects the
scenario, not its position.

## Translate a scenario

Once your quiz serves a second language, language tabs appear on the scenario's title and
description, on the text of each tip, and on the label of each product. The icon of a tip is not
translated: you pick it once for every language. Translations are held and written by the same
**Save** as the rest of the scenario. Adding a language to the quiz is covered in
[Secondary languages](/docs/distribution/secondary-languages).

## Delete a scenario

Select the trash icon on the scenario's row in the left sidebar. The icon does nothing while you
have unsaved changes, with the tooltip **Save or discard your changes first**: save or discard, then
delete.

The confirmation is titled with the scenario's name and reads **Its product trio, tips and
translations will be deleted with it.** and **Recorded quiz responses are kept and will show as
generic results.** Confirm with **Delete scenario**.

The second sentence is the part to weigh before confirming: customers who already reached this
scenario keep their recorded response, and it now displays as a generic result, without the
editorial they originally saw.

## If your plan drops below Basic

Your scenarios are kept. They stay listed on the **Scenarios** tab with all their content, you can still
edit them, and nothing is deleted.

What changes is what the customer sees. Scenarios are no longer served on your online store, so
every customer who finishes the quiz gets the generic result: their products, without any scenario
editorial. One exception: result links already sent by email keep showing their scenario. That
content was already delivered to its customer, and the plan gates the production of new results,
not the reading of old ones.

Move back to Basic or above and your scenarios are served again exactly as they were. There is
nothing to reconfigure.
